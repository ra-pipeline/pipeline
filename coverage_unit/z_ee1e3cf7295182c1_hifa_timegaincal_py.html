7 amptable=None, field=None, spw=None, antenna=None, calsolint=None, targetsolint=None, refant=None, 

8 refantmode=None, solnorm=None, minblperant=None, calminsnr=None, targetminsnr=None, smodel=None,

10 """Determine temporal gains from calibrator observations. 

11 

12 The time-dependent complex gains for each antenna/spw are determined from 

13 the raw data (DATA column) divided by the model (MODEL column), for the 

14 specified fields. The gains are computed according to the spw 

15 mapping/combination, solint, and gaintype as determined in hifa_spwphaseup. 

16 

17 Previous calibrations are applied on the fly. 

18 

19 The process to solve for the various complex gains follows: 

20 

21 - Phase solutions are produced for all intents (excluding the CHECK intent) 

22 using the spw mapping/combination and gaintype determined in 

23 hifa_spwphaseup, and using solint = 'inf' (per scan). The solutions 

24 only registered for the PHASE intent to transfer to itself (PHASE intent) 

25 and the TARGET (and CHECK) intent(s) in hif_applycal. 

26 

27 - Phase (phase-up) solutions are produced for all intents using the spw mapping/ 

28 combination, solint (typically 'int' if not low SNR data) and gaintype as 

29 determined in hifa_spwphaseup. The solutions are used for (1) on-the-fly 

30 application as to solve the subsequent amplitude gains, (2) final phase 

31 correction in hif_applycal of the BANDPASS, AMPLITUDE, DIFFGAIN, 

32 POLARIZATION intents (i.e. after hif_applycal, those intents are 

33 self-calibrated but PHASE and CHECK are not.) These short-solint phases 

34 are also shown as ``diagnostic`` plots. 

35 

36 - Amplitude solutions are produced for all calibrator intents with the above 

37 phase-up solutions pre-applied. The time solint is 'inf' (scan), so solutions 

38 are found for each scan and spw. The solutions are registered for AMP gain 

39 correction of all intents to themselves, and the PHASE intent to the TARGET 

40 and CHECK intent(s). Note: for band-to-band observations, there are no 

41 'high frequency' observations of the PHASE intent, and the full AMP gains are 

42 transferred from the AMPLITUDE intent to the TARGET and CHECK intent(s). 

43 

44 - Short term ``diagnostic`` amplitude solutions are produced for all calibrator 

45 intents using the same short solint as that used for the PHASE intent 

46 phase-up (typically 'int' except for low SNR cases). These solutions are 

47 plotted but not applied. 

48 

49 - Diagnostic phase offsets solutions are produced with solint='inf' for 

50 the BANDPASS and PHASE intent, first solving and preapplying the phase 

51 as a function of time with combine='spw' (to ``zero`` the phases), 

52 and then then solving phase(time) per spw. 

53 Note: by definition the BANDPASS phase will be exactly zero. The phase 

54 solutions for the PHASE intent are used by QA heuristics to identify jumps 

55 and drifts of the spw-spw offsets as a function of time, but if the SNR 

56 is very low, such offsets will not be able to detected. 

57 

58 Good candidate reference antennas were determined using the :func:`~pipeline.hif.cli.hif_refant` task. 

59 During all solutions for standard observing modes, the reference antenna can 

60 change flexibly. For polarization observations a good, un-flagged common 

61 reference antenna is found and locked in time. For band-to-band observations, 

62 all solutions are also made strictly enforcing the use of the selected 

63 reference antenna. 

64 

65 Previous calibrations that have been stored in the pipeline context are 

66 applied on the fly. 

67 

68 The WebLog shows time-series plots of all gains (phase then amplitude), followed by diagnostic Phase 

69 vs. Time plots for the bandpass, flux, polarization, and diffgain calibrators, and the phase calibrator 

70 short-solint solutions. Phase offset plots (one per spw) show the residual per-spw phase offsets vs. 

71 time; if the instrument is stable, these should scatter about zero with no drift. 

72 

73 .. figure:: /figures/PL2025_hifa_timegaincal_allinone.png 

74 :width: 60% 

75 :alt: Gain solution workflow for hifa_timegaincal 

76 

77 Gain solution workflow. Each box shows when ``combine='spw'`` may have been 

78 set, subsequent usage (applycal or diagnostic plotting), and whether 'offset' 

79 solves are used for diagnostic plotting and QA. 

80 

81 Notes: 

82 QA scores for the spw phase offset assessment (per spw, per antenna/polarisation): 

83 

84 - If the standard deviation of all offsets over all antennas (sigma_off) for a spw > 15 deg: QA = 0.82 

85 (too noisy to assess; no further tests). 

86 - Otherwise, QA = 0.5 if any of the following holds: 

87 

88 - Maximum offset > 30 deg or > 6 x sigma_off 

89 - Mean offset > 30 deg or > 6 x sigma_off / sqrt(N_ant) 

90 - Number of scan solutions > 3 and standard deviation of offsets > 30 deg or > 4 x sigma_off 

91 

92 - Otherwise, QA = 0.8 if any of the same tests hold using relaxed limits (15 deg, 5 deg, 15 deg for 

93 max, mean, and std respectively). 

94 - The above scores are decreased by 0.1 if the spw was mapped (phase offsets may affect 

95 calibration). If the spw is not mapped, offsets should calibrate out, and a corresponding 

96 message is shown. 

97 

98 Examples: 

99 1. Compute standard per scan gain solutions that will be used to calibrate the target: 

100 

101 >>> hifa_timegaincal() 

102 

103 """
